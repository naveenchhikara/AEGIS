ABOUT THIS BOOKLET

In credit administration, various aspects related to Pre sanction and Post sanction have to be taken care, to
have healthy portfolio and to avoid risk of failures.
Though operating functionaries are taking lot of care in this aspect, periodical audits play a pivotal role in
gauging the health of the unit and also guide the operating functionaries regarding the importance of systems
and procedures to be followed on an ongoing basis.

Under RFIA, we have various value statements in ORM, CRM and FEMA where scoring/rating is being awarded to Branches,
based on the efficiency in following the Systems & Procedures.

In this booklet, an attempt is made to address all the issues pertaining to Pre- Sanction Areas of Credit Risk Management
(CRM) by providing solutions/guidance along with the related Circular References with Menu Navigations and Solutions. Also,
Share Point Hyper Links have been provided for each Circular/Manual immediately after every Sub_Sub_Parameter for easy
reference by the operating staff.

domain to get maximum score in CRM-Post Sanction Process.

Solutions, Navigations, Circular References:

✓ Overdraft should be sanctioned against the security / as per the product scheme only. Control Return should be submitted to the

Controller for noting.

✓ Entries continuing to be in 98581, due to insufficient balance in customer account, is a cause of concern and more so if the branch is
not able to affect recovery from the customer either because he has stopped transacting in the account or has left the bank/branch.
✓ Overdraft Account to be opened in the home branch of the customer and with same CIF. By opening Overdraft account, the
branches would be able to track the outstanding with the customers, recover interest, take legal action etc. If required in due course,
they can even advise the names of such defaulting customers to CIBIL, so that the recalcitrant or delinquent customers are deprived
of any financial support from other financial institutions.

✓ Branch should mark a lien for the amount involved on customer account in which the excess payment has been made through ATM
and set up auto sweep for overdraft account so that money received in customer account is automatically transferred to OD account.
✓ The account should be opened only after giving due notice about the incident to the customer, and if the recovery has not been

forthcoming even after 30 days of notice.

✓ Proper record of ATM over payments to be maintained at branches.

Solutions, Navigations, Circular References:

✓ The Bank recognizes the importance of Risk Adjusted Return on Capital (RAROC). The pricing decisions are linked to internal risk

rating as well as external rating.

✓ In addition, the RAROC, overall value of relationship and competition in the market would guide pricing decisions. Aspiration by BVs

should be to achieve a minimum aggregate RAROC of 20%.

ROCC/RAROC will be low in the following cases:

✓ If utilization is very low throughout the year and fully utilized at year end.
✓ If borrower has availed only fund-based facilities at concessional rate.
✓ If the borrower rating (ECR) is low, ROCC may appear to be good, but RAROC would be low.
✓ In all the above cases, operating unit needs to provide justification for recommending proposals with RAROC below the indicative

benchmark and a detailed road map on how they propose to achieve the indicative benchmark needs to be drawn up.

• Branches to ensure that the ROI mentioned in the proposal should match with the RAROC computation sheet and as per CBS

records.

• Further, the Primary Security and Collateral details should be mentioned correctly in RAROC computation sheet for LGD calculation.
• Branches should ensure that only the latest template from RMD site is used at the time of computing RAROC and should not use the

old template or share old templates with branches.

Solutions, Navigations, Circular References:
3-23-7. Right of Recompense (ROR)
3-23-7.1 In regard to concessions and reliefs made available to all sick/weak units, a mandatory clause must be stipulated in all the
restructuring packages whereby, when the units turn the corner and the restructuring is successfully completed, the sacrifices undertaken
by the institutions and banks should be recouped from the companies out of their future profits/cash accruals. Alternatively, there may
be provision for equity participation to the extent of sacrifices made.
3-23-7.2 A sacrifice made by the Bank has been defined as the difference between the interest payable at the pre-package rates and actual
rate charged as per that package. The total sacrifice of the Bank would be the sum of the differences so recorded over the package period.
3-23-7.3 In order to safeguard the Bank’s interests, the following should be observed while extending reliefs/concessions under the scheme/
package:
i) The Bank’s right to exercise recompense should be included in the sanction letter to the borrower.
ii) Suitable clause should be added in the sanction letter requiring the unit to show the recompense amount due to the Bank as a contingent
liability in the unit’s balance sheet every year.
iii) The amount of recompense due to the Bank should be advised to the company and their acknowledgement obtained at half-yearly
intervals, which should be kept along with the relative security documents.
iv) In case a decision is taken to call up the advance due to failure of the package, the loss sustained by the Bank on account of
reliefs/concessions extended should also be claimed along with other dues.
Concessions in margin, security, pricing, processing fee, upfront fee etc., if any, to be extended to the customer only after approval from the
competent authority and template approval for the same need to be obtained

Solutions, Navigations, Circular References:

Salient features of the scoring models:

➢ The scoring models will serve as a credit decision-making tool and will strengthen the existing system of sanction or rejection of loan
applications. There are certain assumptions in the scoring models. It is presumed that the minimum eligibility criteria have been
adhered to and therefore, the scoring models do not fix any hurdles rates for such criteria.

➢ For example, no upper age limit is fixed as a hurdle rate in the scores, as it is presumed that loan would not be granted to persons

beyond the upper age limit prescribed or would only begiven after obtaining approval for deviation.

➢ The score range is split into multiple score ranges on a 10 – grade scale, each corresponding to a credit grade, with grade 1 being

the best and grade 10 being the worst in terms of credit behaviour.

➢ Credit scores falling into the same credit score range are to be treated at par. The credit grades corresponding to higher scores are

indicative of better credit behaviour and vice versa.

➢ While proposals which fall in the top score range would be sanctioned, those falling in the bottom range would require to be declined.
➢ For proposals falling in the intermediate range, the proposals may be considered after credit enhancement, (e.g., by selecting the
mode of repayment which carries higher scores, improving Loan amount to Value ratio or Net worth to Loan amount ratio etc.) which
would improve the overall scores and thereby take the scores into clear sanction range.

➢ Credit Score of firm/borrower to be considered as clear/good sanction and to be referred to the next higher authority for

approval/control.

✓ To ensure that the Credit Scoring exercise has been done invariably through LOS/LLMS, while appraising the loan proposal. If any
manual Credit Scoring has been carried out, should be incorporated in the Loan Proposal without fail and to keep the Score Sheet
intact with the documents.

• Liquidity- the liquidity profile of the borrower is adjudged by looking at the seasonally adjusted pattern of funds utilization over the

past 24 months’ period.

• Firmographics- descriptive attributes like maturity of the entity, ownership type, industry, location.
• Repayment Behaviour- Month-on-month analysis of payments made through banking channel along with the asset classification or

Days Past Due are considered to determine if the borrower has a poor or a good track record.

Appraisal of Term Loans:

✓ While appraising a term loan, its prima facie acceptability otherwise should be examined extensively on the same lines as

applicable for any other appraisal.

✓ The purpose of term loan appraisal is to ascertain whether the project is sound - Technically, Economically, Financially, Managerially

and is ultimately viable as a Commercial Proposition.

✓ The ultimate purpose of project appraisal is to ascertain the viability of the project, which has a direct bearing on the repayment
period and the quantum of instalments. While the repayment programme will depend on the profitability of a project, the quantum of
annual instalments to be related to the size of annual cash flows.

✓ The repayment schedule should, therefore, be fixed after ascertaining the annual servicing capacity of the project, during the entire

repayment period, which is indicated by the debt service coverage ratio.

✓ The ‘Internal Rate of Return’ (IRR) is that rate at which the sum of the discounted cash flows is equal to the investment outlay. In
other words, IRR is that rate which makes the present value of the benefits equal to the present value of costs or reduces the net
present value to zero. This ‘Internal Rate of Return Method’ gives the promoters a fair idea about the rate of return that a project is
likely to earn over its useful life.

✓ The break-even point (BEP) has its origins in the economic concept of the “point of indifference”. From an economic perspective, this
point indicates the quantity of some good at which the decision maker would be indifferent i.e., would be satisfied, without reason to
celebrate or to opine. At this quantity, the cost and benefits are precisely balanced. Similarly, the managerial concept of break-even
